<h2 id="share-is-not-rate">A share is not a rate</h2>

The share of registered voters who are young asks: _of everyone on the voters’ roll, how many fall within the stated youth age range?_ The youth registration rate asks: _of eligible young citizens, how many are registered?_ The second calculation needs an eligible-youth denominator. The first does not provide one. <sup><a href="#reference-1" aria-label="Source reference 1">[1]</a></sup>

An age profile of the voters’ roll can show the composition of registration. On its own, it cannot establish the number of unregistered young citizens, the likelihood that a young person will vote, or a youth turnout rate.

**Analytical interpretation:** a small youth share of the roll may reflect population composition as well as registration behaviour. An explanation needs population data, registration definitions and evidence about the mechanisms involved.

<h2 id="age-boundaries">Keep the age boundaries intact</h2>

An observation for ages 18–34 is not an observation for ages 18–35. A published 30–39 group cannot be split into 30–35 and 36–39 without additional information. Proportionally allocating people across those years assumes an age distribution that the source has not established.

MORA preserves published age bands. If a suitable single-year-age dataset becomes available, a documented adapter can aggregate it into the desired range. Until then, the unsupported age-specific metric remains unavailable.

This matters for younger groups, too. Published registration totals may include people who can register before becoming eligible to vote. Check the source’s age rule and the election’s eligibility date before describing every registered person as voting-age eligible.

<h2 id="time-and-place">Match time and place</h2>

Registration totals change as the roll changes. Population estimates refer to a specified date or period and may cover all residents rather than citizens. A defensible youth registration rate requires matched age ranges, geography, legal eligibility and reference dates.

The same test applies to provincial comparisons. A national youth figure cannot be allocated to provinces according to population share and then presented as a provincial observation. A provincial rate needs a provincial numerator and an independently compatible denominator.

**Data availability:** when a compatible eligible-youth population or age-specific registration count is missing, the rate is unavailable. An unavailable rate is not zero, and its absence does not establish low engagement.

<h2 id="responsible-youth-analysis">What responsible analysis can say</h2>

A source-backed age distribution can describe registered voters at its observation date. A matched series can describe how registration changed over time. Compatible eligible-population data can support a registration rate. Age-specific voting evidence can support a participation analysis.

These form a ladder of evidence, not interchangeable measures. Registration creates the possibility of voting; it does not demonstrate that voting occurred.

A useful youth dashboard therefore puts the question before the chart. It tells the reader what is observed, what has been calculated, what would require another dataset, and what remains unknown. That makes gaps in evidence visible without turning them into claims about a generation.
