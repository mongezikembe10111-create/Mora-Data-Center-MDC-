<h2 id="observable-outcome">Start with the observable outcome</h2>

With compatible counts, registered non-participation is the effective election voter population minus the count of participating voters. If the source includes additional voters outside the certified-roll snapshot, preserve the effective election denominator and disclose the distinction. If only ballot-based participation is available, the label must preserve that qualification. The result is an aggregate measure of non-participation under the source’s definitions. <sup><a href="#reference-1" aria-label="Source reference 1">[1]</a></sup>

It is not a survey of intention. It cannot distinguish transport constraints, illness, administrative difficulties, migration, dissatisfaction, disinterest, conflicting commitments or a deliberate political choice.

**Analytical interpretation:** several explanations may fit the same aggregate outcome. A strong explanation identifies evidence that helps distinguish them, rather than selecting a motive from the number alone.

<h2 id="avoid-ecological-inference">Avoid reading individuals into regions</h2>

A municipality with lower turnout does not establish that any particular group living there abstained. Even if the municipality has a large youth population, regional association is not individual evidence. Reading the behaviour of individuals from regional averages risks an ecological inference error.

Survey evidence can help, but it brings its own responsibilities: explain the sample, weighting, wording, fieldwork dates and uncertainty. Election returns and survey responses complement one another because they measure different things.

This also limits political interpretation. A non-voter is not a vote for an unlisted party, a protest vote, or an endorsement of a particular programme. Describing abstention as a specific political preference needs evidence of that preference.

<h2 id="boundaries-and-ballots">Respect boundaries and ballot systems</h2>

Municipal and ward boundaries can change between elections. A place name remaining the same does not guarantee that its geographic footprint or electorate remains the same. Compare boundary vintages and document any harmonisation before describing a long-term geographic trend.

Electoral arrangements also matter. A ward contest, proportional-representation ballot and municipality-wide result can have different coverage. Ballot totals from different contests should not be summed as though they represented different people.

MORA shows only the geographic resolution supported by both data and boundaries. A province value is not a ward value, and a marker on a map is not evidence of a municipal estimate.

<h2 id="legitimacy-and-participation">
  Participation and democratic legitimacy
</h2>

Participation is an important part of democratic life. Yet turnout alone does not measure the quality of rights, institutional accountability, electoral competitiveness, public trust or the fairness of an election.

A decline can be a reason to ask better questions. It is not a complete diagnosis of legitimacy. A defensible assessment considers electoral observation, legal safeguards, political freedoms and direct evidence about public attitudes alongside participation.

**Reporting standard:** present the observed outcome, separate an interpretation from that observation, and identify the additional evidence needed to evaluate the interpretation. When the evidence cannot answer a question, say so.
